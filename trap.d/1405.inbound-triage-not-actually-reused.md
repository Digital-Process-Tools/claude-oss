#1405's own issue text says "inbound_triage.py becomes a fourth source alongside release, curate
and triage" -- but the shipped `inbound` candidate does not call `inbound_triage.py` at all. It
reimplements a coarser metric directly in `scripts/statusline.py` (open issues/pull requests whose
`author_association` is outside the membership tiers), because `statusline.py` is vendored
standalone into managed repos and cannot import sibling `scripts/*.py` modules -- so the "one
module, two consumers" design the maintainer asked for had to live inside `statusline.py` itself.

Observed by the Explore self-review spawn on the #1405/#1406 diff, and it is a real critique: the
vendoring constraint explains why the counting logic could not live in a third shared module, but
it does not explain why the metric had to be coarser than what `inbound_triage.py` already computes
(`classify_pr`'s `green-and-mergeable`/`needs-answer`/`could-not-tell`, `is_inbound_issue`'s
`filed_by_loop`-label-based read) -- `next_action.py` itself is NOT vendored and could import
`inbound_triage.py` directly for its own (non-statusline) ranking candidate, even if the cached,
vendored statusline field stays on the cruder metric. That would mean the two consumers read
slightly different inbound signals rather than the identical one the design note asked for, which
is its own tradeoff to weigh, not obviously the better one. Left as a scope decision for this lane
(closing the "an unanswered pull request renders as nothing-due" gap #1405 named, without also
building the full richer composition), not a bug -- but worth a real design pass rather than staying
implicit.
