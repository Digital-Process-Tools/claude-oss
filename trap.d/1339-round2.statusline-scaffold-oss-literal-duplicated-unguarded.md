scripts/statusline.py:2302 (_VENDORED_DIR_NAME = ".oss") and scripts/scaffold.py:980
(OWNED_DIR = ".oss") are the same fact typed twice. The #1334 fix at
scripts/statusline.py:2366 (`return candidate.parent.name.lower() != _VENDORED_DIR_NAME`)
is the guard that stops a managed repo's vendored .oss/doctor.py from being executed by the
statusline's sys.executable call added in #1314 -- and its correctness rests entirely on
_VENDORED_DIR_NAME staying equal to scaffold.OWNED_DIR. Nothing ties the two:
`grep -rn '_VENDORED_DIR_NAME' tests/` returns zero hits. Change OWNED_DIR without touching
statusline.py and the guard silently stops recognising the vendored directory, trusts the
sibling, and executes an attacker-planted file on session open with no user action.

The asymmetry that makes this reportable rather than theoretical: statusline.py's other
forced duplicate, _safe_which against gh_which.py, got real parity tests in the same delta
(commit c37ad830: test_statusline_safe_which_agrees_with_gh_which_on_a_real_directory and
..._across_real_divergence_shapes). The comment introducing _VENDORED_DIR_NAME explicitly
invokes that same precedent ("the same trade-off _safe_which above already documents") and
then does not follow it. Fix: add the equivalent parity assertion
(test_statusline_vendored_dir_name_agrees_with_scaffold_owned_dir) so the two constants
cannot drift silently.
