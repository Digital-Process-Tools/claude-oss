A task-notification from a sub-manager is not a handback, and the scheduler acted as if it were.

Observed 2026-09-08 in this repo's own scheduler session. The `oss:sub-manager` spawned for the
first tick emitted a `<task-notification>` with `status: completed` whose `<result>` was a real,
`tick_handback.py`-validated `TICK: completed / TICK-ENDS: work-started` message. The scheduler read
that as the tick being over and immediately spawned a second sub-manager for the next tick, per step
7's `work-started` rule.

It was not over. The notification's own note says so plainly -- "A task-notification fires each time
this agent stops with no live background children of its own... the same task-id may notify more than
once" -- and roughly 50 minutes later the same task-id notified again, with a longer handback
describing lanes (#1314, #1318) it had dispatched, watched to green and merged in the interim. So a
`completed` status on the notification means the agent's turn ended, not that the tick ended.

Cost: two sub-managers ran concurrently over the same board for about an hour. Each read the other's
merges as the work of "a concurrent session" or "some other process" and said so in its handback --
the second tick's sub-manager explicitly declined to dispatch a fix for PR #1320's genuinely red legs
because it could not tell whether another live session already owned fix/1314, and it did: the first
sub-manager owned it and fixed it four minutes later. Nothing was lost, but the second tick spent its
context re-deriving a board that was moving under it, and both sub-managers spent turns polling
default-branch shas that the other's merges kept superseding.

What made it hard to see: the first notification's `<result>` was a perfectly well-formed handback.
It classified `completed` under `tick_handback.py` because it *is* a valid handback message. The
absence being read as a presence here is the tick's own continuation -- a sub-manager that has more
to do renders identically, at the notification layer, to one that is finished.

What would have caught it: treating the notification as "this agent's turn ended" and confirming the
tick is closed before spawning the next one -- a `SendMessage` status probe carrying the spawn token,
or simply checking whether the agent is still listed as live, rather than inferring death from a
well-formed final-looking message. `commands/tick.md` step 7 says `work-started` keeps working, and
that is right; what it does not say is that "keeps working" may mean the SAME sub-manager keeps
working, and spawning a second one is not the only reading.
