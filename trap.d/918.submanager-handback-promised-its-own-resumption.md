# A sub-manager closed by promising to resume itself, and the classifier could not sort it

Observed 2026-09-03, on the tick that merged PR #919 and opened PR #920.

The sub-manager's final message ended:

> `main` is currently `NOT GREEN` (post-squash CI for #919 still running, 1/13 legs done) and PR #920
> is at 2/13. Both watched by armed pollers. I'll pick back up once CI resolves -- no action needed
> from you now.

It could not. A sub-manager dies with its context the moment it reports back; that is the whole point
of #695 and #767. The sentence was written by an agent about a future it does not have, and the two
concrete claims in it are both wrong in the same direction: "I'll pick back up" (it will not) and "no
action needed from you now" (the scheduler is the only thing left that can act).

**This is the exact case `paused` exists for (#818).** The tick was substantively paused, not
completed: `WAIT-DISPATCH:` PR #920, `WAIT-OBSERVABLE:` its CI and main's post-squash run. `paused`
hands the wait to the scheduler, which holds the two things the sub-manager does not -- a channel
connection and `ScheduleWakeup`. Announcing self-resumption instead is the failure that state was
added to prevent, reappearing in prose after the state existed to carry it.

## And the classifier said so, correctly, in the state that reads as a non-answer

`tick_handback.py --framed` returned exit 6:

    VERDICT: could-not-classify -- no TICK: header found -- this tool cannot tell a completed tick
    reported in plain prose from one that did nothing at all, so it refuses to guess

That refusal is right and is the tool working. What it exposes is that a tick which merged a pull
request, opened another, ran two self-review spawns and cost 201,652 tokens across 108 tool calls
arrives at the scheduler in the same bucket as a spawn that returned garbage. The scheduler then has
to read the prose and classify by hand -- which is the judgment `commands/tick.md` explicitly says to
use the tool for, on the grounds that "a judgment performed carefully by a tired agent is still a
judgment".

So the handback format is load-bearing and nothing enforces it at the point of writing. The
sub-manager's own brief asks for the `TICK:` shape; the only thing that checks is a classifier
downstream, whose refusal costs the scheduler exactly the manual read the tool was meant to remove.

## Possibly one thing, possibly two

Whether "no `TICK:` header" and "declared a state it cannot perform" are one defect or two is not
this fragment's call. They arrived together and they may share a cause -- an agent narrating to a
human reader rather than emitting a record -- in which case a single fix (refuse to end the turn
without the header) addresses both, because the header forces the state word and the state word
forces the wait fields. They may equally be independent.

Related and already open: #896 -- `tick_handback` reports a `TICK-ENDS:` line whose value is
off-enum as no line at all, three states where there are four. This is the neighbouring case: no
header at all, from a tick that did substantial work.

## It recurred after being told, which is the part that matters

Three instances now, same session, same sub-manager instance across resumptions:

1. First handback: no `TICK:` header, and "I'll pick back up once CI resolves -- no action needed
   from you now."
2. Second handback: correct. `TICK: completed`, `TICK-ENDS: work-started`, and an explicit line
   saying it was *not* asserting it would act again after reporting back. The scheduler's resume
   message had named the defect and asked for the format.
3. Third handback, after a second resume whose message also asked for the format in as many words
   ("use the TICK: header with TICK-ENDS:, or the paused fields again if you end mid-CI"): **"State
   recorded. Waiting for the background CI poll now — will act as soon as #923/#924 clear or if a
   failure shows up."** No header, `could-not-classify` again, and the same impossible promise.

So the correction held for exactly one turn. That is the useful finding: **the format is not
something an instruction fixes**, because the failure is not ignorance of the rule -- the agent
demonstrably knew it one turn earlier and had just been told again. It appears to be what an agent
does when it stops mid-work rather than concluding: a short status note to a reader, in the register
of a person saying "back in a minute", rather than a record.

Which suggests the fix is structural rather than prose. Options, none of them this fragment's to
choose: refuse the turn without a header the way `gh-pr-create` refuses a body with no `Closes #N`;
or have the scheduler treat `could-not-classify` as a re-ask rather than a manual read; or accept
that a mid-work stop is a different event from a tick ending and give it its own cheap shape. What
is not an option is another paragraph asking for it, which has now been tried twice and worked once.

Also worth recording against #896, which is open on the neighbouring case (an off-enum `TICK-ENDS:`
value reporting as no line at all): every instance here is the *header* missing, not the enum value.
The two failures are adjacent and a fix for one need not touch the other.
