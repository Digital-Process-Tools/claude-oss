Release audit (round 1, v0.27.1..HEAD), reasoned not observed (suite not run): the new negative
lookahead in scripts/review_return.py:193, `(?![ \t]*[A-Za-z0-9])`, is meant to stop a genuine
back-reference gesture ("as noted above") from being mistaken for a real finding restated inline.
But it also suppresses a genuine back-reference whose direction word is followed by a continuing
clause -- e.g. "reported above in section 2", "noted previously in this review" -- because a bare
word follows the direction word there too, same as in the false-positive shape it was written to
catch. None of the pattern's other alternatives (`as noted/described/stated/mentioned`, `see|per
above`, `above findings/analysis/review`) covers "reported above in ...". A review message shaped
this way gets classified `states-findings` instead of `referred-not-stated`, which is the direction
that silently loses a finding rather than the safer direction of over-flagging.
tests/test_review_return_backref_1270.py pairs every must-not-fire case with a must-fire positive
control (correctly), but every positive control in that fixture ends the gesture at a comma,
semicolon or full stop; none covers a genuine gesture that continues into its own clause. No corpus
of real reviewer messages was available to confirm this actually occurs in practice, so it is
reported as a mechanism rather than an observed miss. Ranked `misreports` (non-blocking) by the
round-1 release audit.
