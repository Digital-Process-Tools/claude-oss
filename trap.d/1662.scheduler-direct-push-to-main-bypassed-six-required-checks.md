The `/oss:run` scheduler session pushed three commits straight to `main` on 2026-09-18 and the
remote reported a branch-protection bypass, in the push's own stderr:

    remote: Bypassed rule violations for refs/heads/main:
    remote: - 6 of 6 required status checks are expected.

The three commits were two `oss:doctor` repairs from step 1 of the same run (a jit-context
`00-index.tsv` rebuild and an owned `trap.d/README.md` re-apply) plus one `trap.d` fragment commit
written by the scheduler itself. None of them had ever been through a pull request, and the
bypass means none of the six required checks had to pass before they landed on the default branch.

Two separate things worth separating here.

First, the mechanism worked: `git-push` surfaced the bypass line rather than swallowing it, which
is what made this visible at all. `scripts/push_bypass.py` exists to scan a release push's own
receipt for exactly this shape. What has not been checked is whether anything scans an ORDINARY
push's receipt -- this push was not a release push, so `push_bypass.py` was never in the path, and
the only reason the bypass was noticed is that a human-readable line happened to be echoed into a
session that happened to read it.

Second, and the reason this is filed rather than shrugged at: `agents/doctor.md` commits its
repairs directly to the default branch by design, and the scheduler is the session that pushes
them. So this is not a one-off slip by one agent -- it is the standing shape of every `/oss:run`
whose step 1 repairs anything. Every such run puts unreviewed, unchecked commits on `main` and
relies on the post-push CI run to catch what the required checks were supposed to gate.

Not established from here: whether the bypass is because the authenticated account holds an admin
bypass on the protection rule, or because the rule is configured to allow it for this actor. Those
have different fixes (drop the bypass privilege, versus route doctor repairs through a pull request
like every other change) and the ruleset itself was not read.

Cost: one push. The finding was free -- it came out of a receipt that was already being printed.
