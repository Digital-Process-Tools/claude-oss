A `gh-branch` watcher emits `went_not_green` for two states that mean opposite
things, and never emits anything when one becomes the other.

Observed 2026-09-07 on this repo's own `main`. At 01:48:29 the watcher sent
`went_not_green` for `dd64300` with the sentence "nothing has failed, but
`CodeQL` and `tests` have not concluded". That was true when sent. By the time
`tests` concluded, the same commit was NOT GREEN for a different reason -- one
leg had genuinely failed (`pytest (windows-latest, 3.12)`, job 101596981659,
13/14 passed). No second event fired, because the branch's verdict string had
not changed category: it was NOT GREEN before and NOT GREEN after.

Cost: `main` was red for roughly 12 minutes and two further merges (#1209,
#1206) landed on top of it. Nobody was told. It surfaced only because the
scheduler took a direct `gh-branch:SHA` read of a superseded head on the
#968 habit -- "watch the last merge of a tick to conclusion" -- and would
otherwise have gone entirely unobserved, since the next head read GREEN and
the branch-level view had already moved on.

The tell, and it is cheap: a `went_not_green` whose sentence says "nothing has
failed" is a PENDING reading, not a failure reading, and it expires. Treat it as
a promise to look again rather than as a state. The two are one event name today.

Confirmed by reading `gh-branch:dd64300bb10d4300aa20d17dd98e7183c2349acc`
directly after the fact; the failed leg is still there in the commit-mode read
long after the branch moved past it.

Adjacent, same evening, probably the same fix: nothing distinguishes "pending
and might fail" from "concluded and failed" in the branch-level event stream at
all, so a poller consumer cannot tell whether a NOT GREEN branch needs a second
look or a human.
