# One test is 44 seconds of a 4-minute suite

**Observed:** `tests/test_shell_probe.py::test_a_broken_shell_earlier_on_path_does_not_turn_the_suites_red`
takes **43.92s**, top of pytest's `--durations` list. The next slowest are the `test_doctor.py`
subprocess tests at roughly 6.5s each.

**Where it shows:** every local run and every one of CI's 13 legs, so the wall-clock cost is paid
13 times per push.

**What it cost:** nothing incorrect — the test passes and its subject is real (a broken `sh` earlier
on `PATH` must not redden the suite). What it costs is the thing that makes people stop running
tests locally, which is its own kind of expensive.

**Not investigated:** whether the 44s is inherent to what it establishes (building a fake shell,
probing it, and confirming the fallback) or is a sleep, a retry loop, or a subprocess timeout being
waited out. **Do not assume it is padding** — this repository's own rule is that a fixture must
establish the condition it asserts on, and doing that honestly is sometimes slow.

**Unjudged:** this may not be a jit-context rule at all. It looks more like an issue —
"can this test establish the same thing in less time, and if not, say so in its docstring so nobody
re-opens the question." Logged rather than decided.
